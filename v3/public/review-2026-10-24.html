This was a poor day for v3. Its 30 starting model leaders had a summed expected 9.12 wins, but four won. A single day is insufficient to estimate long-term calibration. The original production model remains retained; no probability or prediction has been rewritten.

Probability audit on identical fields

28 races had exactly the starters in the frozen forecast. Late changes in Kelso 15:14, Cheltenham 16:40 and Southwell 20:30 are excluded from these probability metrics, without recalculating their original forecasts.

Market reference: one complete bookmaker grid per race, margin-normalised, observed 10:34–10:46 BST. The model froze at 10:12 BST, so the market had a later observation time. This is descriptive comparison, not a matched-cutoff independent promotion test. Four model winners versus nine market leaders. Top-three accuracy means the winner was model-ranked in the top three, not that a selected horse earned an each-way return.

Where the thinking needs to change

  1. Research and probability were not the same system. The richer comments, full-career context and current card RPR/TS were descriptive additions. They did not alter the frozen learned probabilities.
  2. Core form loses important context. The recent-form statistic uses finishing percentile without beaten margin. Hurdles and chases are grouped together, weakening the distinction between hurdle ability and proven chase execution.
  3. Known warnings were too easy to override. Ashdown Forest's quick return, 6lb rise and weaker card figures were documented, yet the model still estimated 52.1%. Road To Wembley was 58.9% despite a new trip and a rival with no measured hurdle form.
  4. Old marks and conditions stories were overvalued. A previous win off a higher mark did not demonstrate a current revival for Due Date or Desperate Dan. Course/going history did not establish current superiority for Prosperitas.
  5. Do not invent tactical certainty. Several potential leaders do not guarantee a pace collapse. Narrow defeats, jumping errors and unknown debutants require uncertainty, not an explanation that makes every winner appear predictable.

Changes are recorded as hypotheses for chronological testing, not claimed accuracy improvements. This reviewed day is development evidence. The retired June–September test remains closed. No stakes or realised profit are inferred; top-three finishes are not automatically bookmaker-paid places.
