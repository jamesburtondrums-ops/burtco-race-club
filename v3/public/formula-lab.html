FORMULA DEVELOPMENT · 25 OCTOBER 2026

A rebuilt formula, with its gains and failures measured.

The algorithm now learns from prior beaten margins, opposition strength, separate turf/AW/hurdle/chase/bumper records, handicap recency and factual running comments. Odds do not enter the ability model. Seven models were compared across chronological development periods.

What changed inside the formula

Actual previous achievement

A close third and a remote third now differ through beaten margins. Earlier race field size, class and opponents’ ratings provide context. Prior achieved RPR/TS are used only after their race is completed.

Separate racing disciplines

Hurdle success does not become a chase win or chase winning mark. Debut uncertainty, fitness in another code and missing evidence are explicit inputs.

How horses raced

Prior comments supply running style, slow-start, keen-running, obstruction, jumping-error, late-progress and weakening indicators. Rival pace pressure is estimated; a contested lead does not automatically earn a closer a bonus.

Learned combinations

Boosting and conditional ranking learn relationships. A second experiment learned ensemble weights from earlier out-of-fold predictions, rather than assigning fixed handicap or outsider points.

The strongest result is promising, but incomplete

Winner accuracy increased from 27.28% to 28.03%. Log loss and Brier also improved. The development day-bootstrap 95% interval for the accuracy change was +0.05 to +1.44 percentage points. Model selection used these development outcomes, so this interval does not establish an independent prospective gain.

The learned blend found more longer-priced winners within its top three, but fewer as its first choice above 12/1. That failed the recorded development gate. More top-three detections do not automatically mean better each-way bets.

Among horses above 12/1 that were model-ranked in the top three, actual top-three finishes were 509/2582 (19.7%) before and 533/2778 (19.2%) with the blend. Place precision did not improve.

The earlier model already assigned about 2,139 expected wins to the above-12/1 group, where 1,063 actually won. The new blend still overestimated this group. A larger model/market disagreement is not automatically a market error. A blanket outsider boost is unsupported.

SP bands are retrospective diagnostic groups after the predictions were generated. They are not verified early quotes, paid-place terms, betting returns or proof of value. The model never receives SP, favouritism or horse names as fitted ability features.

Checks and next independent evaluation

  • Current-race results, margins, comments, achieved figures and prices cannot change prediction inputs.
  • Every same-day result is embargoed until all that day’s predictions are complete.
  • Separate identities and unambiguous aliases protect against reused horse names and missing country suffixes.
  • The trained challenger produces full-field probabilities and immutable pre-race records; an expired cutoff is rejected.
  • The June–September 2026 test remains retired. October’s reviewed races cannot be reused as unseen evidence.
  • A future period is reserved from 26 October 2026 through 31 January 2027, with at least 1,000 races required. It has not been collected or evaluated here.

No independent accuracy improvement or betting profitability has been demonstrated. Candidate A is preserved as experimental software. The second cycle was rejected under its larger-price criterion. Neither replaces production selections.
